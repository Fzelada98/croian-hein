A complete vertical, and why it cannot be rebuilt.

September 20264 minCroian & Hein

Anyone with money can buy great bottles. A complete vertical requires forty years of somebody else's patience, and that is the part that is not for sale anywhere.

In this note

  2. What is here
  3. What to check before buying one
  4. Who buys them
Write to Bárbara

A vertical is every vintage of one wine, in sequence. A complete vertical is every vintage that was produced, which is not the same thing, and the difference is where the interest lies. The missing years are not gaps; they are the estate's judgement on harvests it chose not to bottle, and a run that includes them would be a run with something wrong in it.

Why it prices above its parts

Auction houses sell cases. Merchants sell bottles. Almost nobody sells sequences, because almost nobody has one, and there are four reasons a complete run is worth more than the sum of the bottles in it.

Time. A run assembled over forty years reflects forty years of buying, one bottle at a time, at the moment each was released. It cannot be compressed. A buyer starting today would spend years and would still be short of the early vintages.

Condition consistency. In a run built by one owner and stored in one cellar, every bottle has the same history. A run assembled from twenty sources has twenty histories, and the weakest one sets the tone.

Completeness is fragile. The value is in the whole. Remove three vintages and what remains is a collection of bottles. That asymmetry is why runs are sold entire and why breaking one is nearly always value-destroying.

Provenance. One owner, one cellar, one chain of custody, which is the strongest provenance statement available.

What to check before buying one

  1. Is it genuinely complete? Complete means every vintage produced. Ask which years were not made, and

why. If the seller cannot answer, they do not know the wine.

  1. One owner or many? Ask where it was assembled and over what period. A run bought as a run last year is

a different object from a run built over four decades.

  1. Condition, bottle by bottle. Ask for the fill level of every bottle over

thirty years old, individually. A summary of "all excellent" is not a description.

  1. Original cases. Where the wine ships in cases, as Masseto does, ask whether they are

sealed. See the note on original cases.

  1. How it will travel. Fifty-two bottles is a consignment, not a parcel, and it moves once. The

transport note sets out what to insist on.

Who buys them

In practice, three kinds of buyer, and they want different things.

Collectors who want the object itself and have no intention of opening most of it. For them completeness is the whole point and a single missing year is disqualifying.

Restaurants with serious lists, which buy a run to pour verticals by the glass or to offer a guest their birth year for the next thirty years. These buyers care about the modern half and about reliable condition.

Family offices, holding wine as one line in a patrimony. They care about documentation, storage and eventual resale, which is the subject of the note on wine as a Swiss asset.

Questions we are asked
What is a complete wine vertical?

Every vintage of one wine that was actually produced, in sequence. Years in which the estate made no wine are absent by definition, and a run that appears to include them is a warning sign.

Why is a vertical worth more than the individual bottles?

Because it cannot be compressed. It reflects decades of buying at release, a single storage history, one chain of custody, and a completeness that is destroyed by removing any part of it.

Should a vertical ever be split?

Rarely. The value sits in the whole, and a run with three vintages removed is a collection of bottles. Splitting is almost always value-destroying.

What should I ask before buying a complete vertical?

Which vintages were never made and why, who assembled it and over what period, the fill level of every bottle over thirty years old individually, whether original cases are sealed, and how it will travel.
